# Photography Course Presentation Script

## Slide 1 — Seeing with Intention
“Hi everyone, today I’ll present a simple framework for making stronger photos.  
My focus is on three core skills: composition, light, and storytelling.  
If we apply these intentionally, our images become clearer and more memorable.”

## Slide 2 — What Makes a Photo Memorable?
“A memorable photo usually does three things well.  
First, it creates an emotional response.  
Second, it is technically clear enough that the viewer is not distracted.  
Third, it has a strong visual focus, so we know where to look immediately.”

## Slide 3 — Composition Basics
“Composition is how we organize visual elements in the frame.  
The rule of thirds helps place subjects away from the center for balance.  
Leading lines guide the viewer’s eye to the subject.  
Framing and negative space simplify the image and strengthen the main idea.”

## Slide 4 — Light as the Main Tool
“Light affects mood more than any camera setting.  
Soft light gives gentle shadows and flattering portraits.  
Hard light creates contrast and drama.  
Direction also matters: side light adds texture, while backlight can add depth.  
For beginners, golden hour and window light are very reliable.”

## Slide 5 — Camera Settings that Matter Most
“Aperture controls depth of field, so it affects background blur.  
Shutter speed controls motion: faster speeds freeze action, slower speeds show movement.  
ISO controls brightness but also adds noise at higher values.  
A practical approach is to choose the creative priority first, then adjust the other two settings.”

## Slide 6 — Storytelling Through a Photo Series
“One photo can be strong, but a series can tell a richer story.  
A simple sequence is: wide shot for context, medium shot for action, and detail shot for emotion.  
Keeping a consistent subject and mood helps the viewer follow the narrative.”

## Slide 7 — Common Beginner Mistakes
“Three common issues are easy to fix.  
First, cluttered backgrounds compete with the subject.  
Second, missed focus weakens impact.  
Third, flat midday light can make scenes look dull.  
Small adjustments in angle, focus point, and time of day can improve results quickly.”

## Slide 8 — Before/After Case Study
“Here is an example of improvement.  
In the ‘before’ image, the background is busy and the subject blends in.  
In the ‘after’ image, I changed angle, simplified the background, and used side light.  
The subject is now clear, and the photo communicates a stronger mood.”

## Slide 9 — Practical 7-Day Improvement Plan
“To improve fast, practice with a short routine for one week.  
Each day, shoot one theme such as lines, light, texture, or emotion.  
At the end of each day, select one best image and one image to improve.  
This builds both shooting and editing judgment.”

## Slide 10 — Conclusion + Q&A
“To summarize, better photos come from intentional choices in composition, light, and story.  
You don’t need complex gear to improve—just consistent practice and clear decisions.  
Thank you for listening, and I’m happy to take your questions.”
