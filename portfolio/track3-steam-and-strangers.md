### Prague's Old Kavárna Windows

## Concept (narrow, as required)

The condensation-fogged windows of Prague's historic cafés — Slavia, Café Louvre, Café
Imperial, Kavárna Obecní dům — as **membranes between the warm interior life and the
cold street**. Anonymous figures, glowing lamplight, blurred gestures, and the breath
of the room written on the glass. The theme is deliberately tight: not "cafés," not
"Prague life," but *one pane of fogged glass and the strangers behind it*.

## Emotional thesis

> *Connection seen through a veil — we are always one pane of glass apart.*

## Visual grammar

- **Palette:** color — **warm interior light against cold exterior blue**; the
  temperature contrast carries the whole emotional argument.
- **Time of day:** **winter dusk**, when interiors glow and the street goes cold and
  dark, maximizing condensation and the warm/cold split.
- **Focus:** intentional **blur and condensation used as a creative filter** —
  figures are felt, not identified.
- **Recurring device:** the glass as a screen between *us* (cold, outside) and
  *them* (warm, inside); reflections of the street laid over the patrons.

## 10-image sequence

| # | Image | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A glowing café front seen from across the dark street. | Establishing warmth, from the cold. |
| 2 | A fogged window with a single silhouette. | One stranger, unreachable. |
| 3 | A hand wiping a clear arc in the condensation. | The wish to see / be seen. |
| 4 | Two figures mid-conversation, unreadable through the steam. | Intimacy we are outside of. |
| 5 | Reflection of the street/lamps superimposed on the patrons. | Our world laid over theirs. |
| 6 | A close detail: rivulets of condensation running down glass. | The veil itself, time passing. |
| 7 | A waiter's blurred passage, motion through the fog. | Life continuing without us. |
| 8 | An empty cup and chair behind the glass. | Absence; someone has left. |
| 9 | A face turned toward the window, eyes unclear. | The near-meeting of two gazes. |
| 10 | The café door opening, warmth and light spilling onto the cold street. | The veil breached — connection, briefly. |

## Shooting notes

- **Discretion and ethics:** shoot from the public street; favor silhouettes,
  blur, and condensation so individuals are not identifiable. Do not photograph
  recognizable faces without consent; the fog is both the subject and the consent
  mechanism.
- **Light:** expose for the warm interior highlights and let the cold exterior fall
  dark; the steam naturally diffuses the lamps into glows.
- **Lens:** fast 35–85 mm equiv. at f/1.8–2.8 to gather low light and isolate
  figures; a longer reach helps keep a respectful distance.
- **Weather:** go on cold, damp evenings — the colder the street, the heavier the
  condensation. Bring a cloth; move between 3–4 cafés in one dusk window.

## Artist Statement — draft (≈300 words, one page)

> *Write in first person, present tense; evocative, not descriptive. Edit to fit one page.*

In winter I walk the cold streets and the cafés glow like lanterns I am not inside of.
The glass fogs with the breath of everyone in the warm room, and that fog becomes a
veil — I can see that there is life in there, laughter, a hand around a cup, but I
cannot quite reach it. This is the feeling I have chased my whole life and never named:
to be one pane of glass away from belonging.

So this portfolio is not about cafés. It is about the veil itself, and the strangers
who are always just behind it.

I shoot only at winter dusk, in color, because the whole emotion lives in temperature
— the warm amber inside, the cold blue street where I stand. I let the condensation do
what a lens never could: it blurs faces into feelings, turns people into the idea of
people, and protects their privacy while it deepens my longing. I photograph the
reflections of the street sliding across the patrons, my world laid quietly over
theirs. Nothing is sharp, because connection across glass never is.

The ten images move from outside in. We arrive cold, drawn to a glowing front. We meet
a single silhouette, then a hand wiping a small clear window in the fog — the wish to
see and be seen. We watch a conversation we cannot enter, our own reflection laid over
it, the steam running down like time. A waiter passes; a cup sits abandoned; a face
turns toward the glass and almost meets ours. And then, at the end, the door opens —
warmth and light spill onto the cold stone — and for one breath the veil is gone.
