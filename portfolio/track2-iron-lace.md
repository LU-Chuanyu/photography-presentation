# Track 2 — Single Urban Element: *Prague's Cast-Iron Grilles & Railings*
### "Iron Lace"

> **Recommended track** — most tightly focused, most visually distinctive, lowest
> cliché risk.

## Why this motif

Prague's Art Nouveau and Baroque ironwork — window grilles, door railings, balcony
balustrades, gate scrolls — is everywhere and almost never *seen*. It is a tangible,
endlessly repeatable subject with infinite variation, which makes it ideal for a
tightly focused series. The motif is also quietly loaded: iron lace is decoration
wrapped around a barrier, beauty whose job is to keep you out.

## Emotional thesis

> *Beauty built to keep the world out — intimacy and barrier in the same gesture.*

## Visual grammar

- **Palette:** high-contrast **black-and-white**, abstracting the iron into pure
  line and shadow.
- **Composition:** tight, **frame-filling crops**; the iron rarely sits in obvious
  architectural context.
- **Light:** **hard directional light** to throw the grille's shadow onto walls and
  floors — the motif duplicated as shadow, restating "what is kept in vs. out." One
  overcast pass for even-toned detail frames.
- **Recurring device:** the grille as a screen we are always looking *through* or
  *against*, never simply *at*.

## 10-image sequence

The order is authored. Read it as: specimen → DNA → presence as absence → the human
kept inside → decay → the city as rhythm → memory bending → line in light → the
barrier failing → release.

| # | Image | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | A full doorway grille, near-symmetrical, frame-filling. | The "specimen" — establishes the subject. |
| 2 | Extreme detail: a single floral iron curl. | The motif's DNA. |
| 3 | Shadow of a grille across cobblestones, the iron itself out of frame. | Presence as absence. |
| 4 | A grille with a hand / curtain / plant just visible behind it. | The human kept inside. |
| 5 | Rust and peeling-paint macro. | Decay, time. |
| 6 | Repetition: three balconies stacked, identical ironwork. | The city as rhythm. |
| 7 | A grille distorted by reflection in old, warped glass. | Memory bending. |
| 8 | Backlit silhouette of an ornate gate at dusk. | A line drawing made of light. |
| 9 | A broken or bent bar. | The barrier failing. |
| 10 | A grille framing a sliver of sky or street beyond. | Release / the world outside. |

## Shooting notes

- **Two sessions:** one **overcast day** for even tones on the detail and texture
  frames (1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7), and one **harsh-sun afternoon** purely for shadow-play
  frames (3, 8).
- **Lens:** a **50 mm / 85 mm equivalent** to compress and isolate; deliberately
  avoid wide lenses that drag in clichéd street context.
- **Aperture:** f/5.6–f/8 for the flat detail frames; open up to f/2.8 for frame 4 so
  the iron stays sharp while the interior life behind it softens.
- Carry a small step or shoot from across the street with the tele end to get clean,
  square-on grille faces without keystoning.
- For frame 9, scout neglected courtyards and back lanes (e.g., Žižkov, Holešovice,
  Nové Město side streets) where iron is left to fail.

## Location & lighting schedule

| Session | Time | Light | Target frames |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Overcast | Any even-light day | Soft, shadowless | 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7 |
| B — Hard sun | Mid-to-late afternoon, low raking sun | Strong cast shadows | 3, 8 |
| Scout | Ongoing while walking | Any | 9, 10 |

Good hunting grounds: the Art Nouveau facades around **Josefov** and **Vinohrady**,
Baroque gates in **Malá Strana**, and decaying ironwork in **Žižkov** courtyards.

## Artist Statement — draft (≈300 words, one page)

> *Write in first person, present tense; evocative, not descriptive. Edit to fit one page.*

I started noticing the iron the way you notice a held breath. Prague hangs lace over
its windows and doors — curling, flowering, impossibly delicate — and then asks that
lace to do the work of a wall. Every grille in this city is a contradiction I can hold
in one frame: someone wanted beauty, and someone wanted to be kept safe, and they
decided not to choose.

This portfolio is about that double gesture. Not the buildings, not the addresses —
the threshold itself, the moment where ornament becomes barrier.

I work entirely in black-and-white because color would let you off the hook; it would
make these pretty. Stripped to line and shadow, the iron stops being decoration and
becomes structure, intention, a drawn boundary. I crop tight and square so there is no
escape into "a charming street in Prague" — only the screen, and us on the wrong side
of it. I chase hard afternoon light so each grille throws a second self onto the
stone: the shadow is the thing it keeps out, the proof that the barrier works even
when no one is touching it.

The ten images move from the whole to the wound. We meet a single grille head-on, fall
into one iron curl, then watch the iron disappear and leave only its shadow. We glimpse
a hand behind the bars, the human the lace protects. We pass through rust, repetition,
and warped reflection — the city aging — until a bar finally gives way. The series ends
on a grille that frames not an interior but a sliver of open sky, as if the thing built
to keep the world out has, at last, framed a way through.
