# Track 1 — One Location: *Vyšehrad*
### "The Quiet Rock Above the River"

## Why this site

Vyšehrad is Prague's mythic origin-place — the rock where, by legend, Princess Libuše
stood and prophesied a city "whose glory would touch the stars." Yet today it is a
hushed, slightly melancholic hilltop: a fortress that guards memory rather than
territory. It holds the national cemetery (Slavín), a Neo-Gothic basilica, and long
ramparts looking down on the Vltava. It is far less clichéd than Charles Bridge or
Old Town Square, and its quiet makes it ideal for subjective, metaphorical work.

## Emotional thesis

> *A place where the city goes to be quiet and remember its dead.*

## Visual grammar

- **Palette:** cold, desaturated color overall, with **selective black-and-white**
  reserved for the cemetery frames (memory rendered as monochrome).
- **Light / time of day:** **early-morning fog** and **blue hour at dusk** — never
  flat midday sun.
- **Composition:** heavy **negative space** and **silhouettes**; slightly **low /
  looking-up angles** to evoke reverence and scale.
- **Recurring device:** the threshold — gates, arches, and tree-frames that separate
  "the city" from "the other, quiet place."

## 10-image sequence

The order is authored. Read it as: leaving the city → entering memory → the personal
and the collective dead → solitude and the unreachable city → time as material →
myth → return, transformed.

| # | Image | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Wide, foggy approach to the Gothic gate (Špička / Táborská brána). | The threshold; the city left behind. |
| 2 | Detail: worn stone steps / a single shaft of light. | Entry into the "other" space. |
| 3 | Spires of the Basilica of SS. Peter & Paul emerging from mist. | The mythic axis. |
| 4 | Slavín cemetery — a single grave detail, shallow focus (B&W). | Memory becomes personal. |
| 5 | Repeating headstone rhythm receding into haze (B&W). | Collective memory. |
| 6 | A lone figure or empty bench on the ramparts, back to camera. | Solitude. |
| 7 | The Vltava far below, seen through tree branches. | The city seen but unreachable. |
| 8 | Abstracted texture: lichen, iron, cracked masonry. | Time as material. |
| 9 | Silhouetted statue against a bruised dusk sky. | Myth made monumental. |
| 10 | Departing gate at near-dark, lamp just lit. | Return, transformed. |

## Shooting notes

- **Two visits:** a foggy dawn (frames 1–3, 8) and a blue-hour dusk (frames 6, 9, 10);
  the cemetery (4–5, 7) works in soft overcast light at either visit.
- **Tripod** for all low-light frames; cable release / 2-second timer.
- **Aperture:** f/8 for the architectural rhythm shots (3, 5, 9); f/2–2.8 for the
  grave and texture details (4, 8) to isolate the subject.
- **Lens:** a normal-to-short-tele range (35–85 mm equiv.) keeps frames intimate and
  avoids the wide "postcard" look; one wider frame is acceptable for the establishing
  shot (1).
- Keep ISO low and embrace long exposures so fog and water render as soft veils.

## Location & lighting schedule

| Session | Time | Light | Target frames |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A — Dawn | Arrive 30 min before sunrise | Fog, cold blue, first shaft of light | 1, 2, 3, 8 |
| B — Dusk | Arrive 45 min before sunset, stay past blue hour | Warm-to-cold transition, lamps lighting | 6, 9, 10 |
| Either | Soft overcast midday window | Even, low-contrast | 4, 5, 7 |

Check the fog forecast and sunrise/sunset times the night before; reschedule Session A
if there is no humidity/fog (the mist is structural to the thesis).

## Artist Statement — draft (≈300 words, one page)

> *Write in first person, present tense; evocative, not descriptive. Edit to fit one page.*

I keep returning to the rock above the river when I want the city to be quiet. Down
in the streets Prague performs itself; up here, behind the old gate, it stops
performing and starts remembering. Vyšehrad does not feel like a fortress that keeps
enemies out — it feels like a place that keeps the dead in, gently, the way you hold a
name you are afraid to forget.

So this is not a portfolio about a monument. It is about the feeling of crossing a
threshold and finding the city has gone silent behind you.

Every choice serves that silence. I shoot at dawn fog and at blue hour because those
are the hours when stone loses its edges and turns to memory; midday would make
Vyšehrad a tourist fact, and I want a mood. I keep the palette cold and nearly
colorless, and I let the cemetery fall fully into black-and-white — grief, to me, has
no color, only weight. I shoot low and let the spires and statues rise out of the
frame so that myth stays larger than I am. I crop tight on a single grave, a patch of
lichen, a bend of rusted iron, because remembering is never general; it is always one
name, one hand, one stone.

The ten images are ordered as a passage. We leave the bright city at the gate, enter
the fog, meet first the personal dead and then the countless ones, stand alone on the
ramparts with the unreachable river below, touch time where it has worn into the
walls, and finally turn back toward a gate just as the first lamp is lit. We return to
the city we left — but quieter now, and carrying something we did not bring in.
